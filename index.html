RESEARCH UPDATE · OCTOBER 11, 2026

What the evidence can tell us.

Historical findings can improve the questions we ask. They do not establish a new dispatch, sampling or reopening rule.

Operational improvement not established

Displayed forecasts come from NOAA. Experimental fitted models remain research-only.

No experimental model supplies the current numbers on this page, and no automated model warning is running here. Historical improvements have not satisfied the combined Samish requirements for missed rises, false alerts and usable crest uncertainty.

The forecast-change check uses fixed rules rather than a learned model: it compares a later gauge reading with an earlier NOAA forecast shifted once to meet the gauge. It says “above the earlier NOAA forecast and still rising” only when the difference is at least 10% of the seasonal rise number and the measured near-three-hour change is positive. If the difference clears that line but the measured change is not positive, it says “above the earlier NOAA forecast, but not rising now.” That line was fixed before future events, but no future-event evidence yet shows useful warning time or an acceptable false-comparison rate.

SAMPLING TIMING

Timing is a real opportunity.

21% near a gauge maximum identified by looking backward

Of 787 timed storm samples across 153 episodes, 165 were within the study’s ±3-hour window; 398 (51%) were more than three hours after it.

For 199 forecasted crests, a NOAA issue available at least 12 hours beforehand put the high within ±6 hours on 39%; the typical absolute timing error was 8.8 hours.

The maximum was identified within ±24 hours of sampling. The forecast was read after the true high was known and anchored to that high, so this is an optimistic upper bound—not a dispatch rule.

LATER RESAMPLING

Some later pairs passed. The evidence is small.

7 / 19 initially failing days had a passing later pair

These days span 12 episodes. A resampling calculation that kept each storm episode together produced a broad plausible range of approximately 12–61%; the analysis did not distinguish rescue from ordinary sampling variability.

This is not a dependable resampling rule or a promise that another trip will succeed.

EARLIER-FORECAST COMPARISON

The comparison begins as a measuring tool.

The page can compare the gauge with an earlier NOAA forecast that this site recorded during a prior check on the device. This directly records the kind of forecast-versus-reality divergence Jason described.

No future storm outcomes have scored the comparison yet. Before it becomes an alert, the event record must show its warning time, missed rises and false watches.

DOPPLER RADAR

Useful for checking surprise; not a simple correction by itself.

The Outlook places official KATX base reflectivity, estimated one-hour accumulation and estimated storm-total accumulation beside the river graph. When available, the outline is the simplified drainage area upstream of gauge 12201500 based on whole NHDPlusV2 catchments—not the full Samish watershed. These views can show where precipitation echoes and radar-estimated accumulation occur, but the page does not turn them into area-wide rainfall or feed them into the river forecast.

Radar alone does not convert echo location and intensity into watershed runoff, and it does not account for soil saturation or the delay before water reaches the gauge. The earlier Multi-Radar/Multi-Sensor rain feature test did not beat the strongest anchored NOAA reading, so radar remains context rather than a predictive correction.

How to read these findings

The numbered summaries above are aggregate results from the Site 32 resampling study and an analysis of sample timing relative to river highs. They are not current observations. Sample-level records and laboratory data are not distributed here.

Studies use different samples, event definitions and time periods. Counts cannot be pooled into one model accuracy figure. Historical evaluation is separate from a future-season test. These summaries are project research, not provider endorsement.

KNOW WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Sources & freshness.

Observation time, forecast issue time and page retrieval time mean different things. Feeds come from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), and the National Weather Service (NWS). All displayed times are Pacific.

Display rules

  • Gauge readings more than 2 hours old are marked stale. The last measurement stays visibly dated; it is not called current.
  • River forecasts more than 48 hours old are withheld from the outlook. This is a page display limit, not proof of forecast accuracy.
  • The raw NOAA forecast stops at its actual end. Lines fill only between supplied points no more than 6 hours apart; they never extend beyond the data. If NOAA’s first future value is after this page’s check time, that gap stays visible and no check-time value is invented.
  • An eligible earlier forecast may appear as its own chart line after one disclosed gauge alignment. It never replaces or silently shifts NOAA’s current raw path.
  • The KATX radar products come directly from the National Weather Service. Reflectivity shows echoes aloft; the one-hour and storm-total products are radar estimates in inches. The exact product time is displayed. None drives a river, closure, or safety calculation. If its official legend fails, the page labels that failure and directs the user to the full radar.
  • The outline is the simplified drainage area upstream of USGS gauge 12201500 returned by the U.S. Geological Survey Network Linked Data Index and based on whole NHDPlusV2 catchments. It is not the full Samish watershed and is not a property, harvest-area, or regulatory boundary.
  • Each current forecast grid-cell polygon is checked against that gauge-upstream reference before its value appears. The point is withheld if either geometry is invalid or unavailable, or if the full cell is not inside the reference. These remain point forecasts—not an area average.
  • The experimental rise screen needs a complete 24-hour gauge path, gauge gaps no longer than one hour, at least 60 hours of forecast reach, and a valid point where the gauge and forecast overlap. It shifts the forecast by one constant, then reports the largest rise relative to the seasonal reference in effect at that time. A larger raw rise can exist across a seasonal boundary. It is not the Department of Health calculation.
  • The forecast-change check keeps the first view of each NOAA issue on this device for 14 days. It requires a forecast from 3–36 hours earlier, no path gap over 12 hours, and the observation nearest three hours earlier when it falls within 2.5–3.5 hours. The history can be lost when site data are cleared. Updates occur only while the page is on screen; this page sends no notifications.
  • Rain forecasts more than 24 hours old are marked stale. Six-hour blocks appear only with full coverage; both points must cover the complete next 24 hours for the headline mean.
  • Observed station rain is a provisional rolling total from automated gauges. It is not an hourly series, not basin-wide rain, and not numerically compared with forecast points at other locations.
  • Storm checks are created only when the user presses Save. On the next save, records older than 90 days are removed and only the newest 500 are kept. They remain on this device and can be exported or deleted.
  • Missing values remain unavailable. Zero means a numeric zero was supplied.
  • A failed update removes that source’s prior values. A source failure never becomes “low risk.”
