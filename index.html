RESEARCH UPDATE · OCTOBER 11, 2026

What the evidence can tell us.

Historical findings can improve the questions we ask. They do not establish a new dispatch, sampling or reopening rule.

Operational improvement not established

Live forecasts above come from NOAA. The AI stays in research.

A frozen neural model is being logged in a local shadow trial. It sends no alerts and supplies none of the live numbers on this page. Historical improvements have not satisfied the combined Samish requirements for missed rises, false alerts and usable crest uncertainty.

SAMPLING TIMING

Timing is a real opportunity.

21% near a retrospectively identified gauge maximum

Of 787 timed storm samples across 153 episodes, 165 were within the study’s ±3-hour window; 398 (51%) were more than three hours after it.

The maximum was identified within ±24 hours of sampling. The ±3-hour definition is a research choice, not Jason’s established tolerance or a tested dispatch target.

LATER RESAMPLING

Some later pairs passed. The evidence is small.

7 / 19 initially failing days had a passing later pair

These days span 12 episodes. The episode-bootstrap interval is approximately 12–61%; the analysis did not distinguish rescue from ordinary sampling variability.

This is not a dependable resampling rule or a promise that another trip will succeed.

BACTERIA MODEL

Useful historical signal at sample time.

A model evaluated on 107 confirmation samples had AUC 0.769 and Brier score 0.176, compared with 0.193 for the seasonal model (lower Brier is better).

It uses flow observed at sampling and assumes earlier lab results are available after at least two days. AUC is not percent accuracy. This is not an advance contamination forecast.

REOPENING RESEARCH

A simple rule was the stronger baseline.

A transparent first-sample rule had 12-hour mean absolute error across 15 historical confirmation episodes, versus 18 hours for a simple baseline. More complex models did not improve on the rule during development; the confirmation comparison used the rule and baseline.

Nine of those 15 reopening clocks were imputed. No reopening countdown or permission to harvest follows from this result.

How to read these findings

These are aggregate summaries of the Site32 resampling study, hydrograph-phase analysis and frozen bacteria/reopening research. They are not live observations. Sample-level records and laboratory data are not distributed here.

Studies use different samples, event definitions and time periods. Counts cannot be pooled into one model accuracy figure. Historical evaluation is separate from a future-season test. These summaries are project research, not provider endorsement.

KNOW WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Sources & freshness.

Observation time, forecast issue time and page retrieval time mean different things. All displayed times are Pacific.

Display rules

  • Gauge readings more than 2 hours old are marked stale. The last measurement stays visibly dated; it is not called current.
  • River forecasts more than 48 hours old are withheld from the outlook. This is a page display limit, not proof of forecast accuracy.
  • The forecast stops at its actual end. Lines interpolate only between supported points no more than 6 hours apart; they never extend beyond the data.
  • Rain forecasts more than 24 hours old are marked stale and excluded from the headline. Both points must cover the complete next 24 hours for a two-point total.
  • Missing values remain unavailable. Zero means a numeric zero was supplied.
  • A failed refresh removes the failed feed’s prior values. Feed failures do not turn into “low risk.”
