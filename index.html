What it is

An estimate of the chance that the Samish River rises fast enough within the next 3 days to cross the shellfish-harvest trigger at the Burlington gauge. The trigger is a rise of a set size within 24 hours: the flow now minus the flow 24 hours earlier. Next to it you get NOAA's own river forecast, the rain forecast and the tide, all on one time axis.

The chance is ours, not NOAA's forecast repackaged. It is a calibrated reading of NOAA's river forecast. We take the measured flow for the past 24 hours, join NOAA's latest forecast path on the end, and find the largest 24-hour rise anywhere in the next 72 hours. That rise, divided by the trigger, goes through a two-term logistic curve fitted on Oct 2018 – Sep 2024. It was then checked on Oct 2024 – Sep 2026, which it never saw (see Track record). NOAA tends to over-forecast rises on this river: a forecast rise exactly at the trigger has historically meant roughly a 3-in-10 chance.

Where each number comes from

  • Gauge Flow: USGS 12201500, Samish River near Burlington, provisional 15-minute readings. Live from the USGS Water Data API, backed by NOAA's copy of the same gauge.
  • NOAA River forecast: Northwest River Forecast Center, point SMRW1, through the National Water Prediction Service. It is usually issued once a day, in 6-hour steps.
  • NOAA Rain: National Weather Service gridded precipitation forecast, averaged over two points in the basin (near Alger and Sedro-Woolley).
  • NOAA Tide: CO-OPS predictions at Bellingham (9449211), the nearest prediction station to Samish Bay. Times and heights at Samish Bay itself will differ somewhat.
  • Estimate Chance and hour strip: ours, as described above. They recompute on every refresh (every 10 minutes while this page is open). The estimate is withheld if NOAA's forecast is more than 48 hours old.

The trigger

By default the trigger changes with the season: +400 cfs in 24 h from November to February, +200 in March and April, and +100 from May to October. This is our working schedule, not a published rule; tell us if it is wrong. You can override it under "Your lines".

What it is not

  • Not a closure prediction. The Washington Department of Health makes all closure and reopening decisions. No Department of Health records, sampling results or bacteria numbers are used or shown on this page.
  • No crest-window or "has it crested" probability yet. Crest numbers on this page are NOAA's forecast, labelled as such.
  • Two test years hold 26 crossings. That is a thin record; the percentages carry real uncertainty.
