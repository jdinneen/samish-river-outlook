RESEARCH UPDATE · OCTOBER 11, 2026

What the evidence can tell us.

Historical findings can improve the questions we ask. They do not establish a new dispatch, sampling or reopening rule.

Operational improvement not established

Live forecasts above come from NOAA. Experimental fitted models remain research-only.

No experimental model supplies the live numbers on this page, and no automated model warning is running here. Historical improvements have not satisfied the combined Samish requirements for missed rises, false alerts and usable crest uncertainty.

The saved-forecast check uses fixed rules rather than a learned model: it compares a later gauge reading with a NOAA path shifted once to meet the gauge and stored 3–36 hours earlier. It marks “outpacing” only when the difference is at least 10% of the seasonal rise number and the measured near-three-hour change is positive. That line was fixed before future events, but no future-event evidence yet shows useful warning time or an acceptable false-watch rate.

SAMPLING TIMING

Timing is a real opportunity.

21% near a gauge maximum identified by looking backward

Of 787 timed storm samples across 153 episodes, 165 were within the study’s ±3-hour window; 398 (51%) were more than three hours after it.

For 199 forecasted crests, a NOAA issue available at least 12 hours beforehand put the high within ±6 hours on 39%; the typical absolute timing error was 8.8 hours.

The maximum was identified within ±24 hours of sampling. The forecast was read after the true high was known and anchored to that high, so this is an optimistic upper bound—not a dispatch rule.

LATER RESAMPLING

Some later pairs passed. The evidence is small.

7 / 19 initially failing days had a passing later pair

These days span 12 episodes. A resampling calculation that kept each storm episode together produced a broad plausible range of approximately 12–61%; the analysis did not distinguish rescue from ordinary sampling variability.

This is not a dependable resampling rule or a promise that another trip will succeed.

SURPRISE WATCH

The new comparison begins as a measuring tool.

Each browser can preserve the first view it received of a NOAA forecast issue, then compare that saved path with a later gauge reading. This directly records the kind of forecast-versus-reality divergence Jason described.

No future storm outcomes have scored the watch yet. Before it becomes an alert, the event record must show its warning time, missed rises and false watches.

DOPPLER RADAR

Useful for checking surprise; not a simple correction by itself.

Radar can show that rain is falling harder or lasting longer than expected, so the Outlook links directly to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) radar view. The page does not yet calculate rainfall over the Samish watershed or feed radar into the river forecast.

A radar view alone does not convert rain location and intensity into watershed runoff, and it does not account for soil saturation or the delay before water reaches the gauge. Those pieces need an issued-time historical test before they drive a warning.

How to read these findings

The numbered summaries above are aggregate results from the Site 32 resampling study and an analysis of sample timing relative to river highs. They are not live observations. Sample-level records and laboratory data are not distributed here.

Studies use different samples, event definitions and time periods. Counts cannot be pooled into one model accuracy figure. Historical evaluation is separate from a future-season test. These summaries are project research, not provider endorsement.

KNOW WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Sources & freshness.

Observation time, forecast issue time and page retrieval time mean different things. Feeds come from NOAA, the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), and the National Weather Service (NWS). All displayed times are Pacific.

Display rules

  • Gauge readings more than 2 hours old are marked stale. The last measurement stays visibly dated; it is not called current.
  • River forecasts more than 48 hours old are withheld from the outlook. This is a page display limit, not proof of forecast accuracy.
  • The raw NOAA forecast stops at its actual end. Lines fill only between supplied points no more than 6 hours apart; they never extend beyond the data.
  • The experimental rise screen needs a complete 24-hour gauge path, gauge gaps no longer than one hour, at least 60 hours of forecast reach and a valid point where the gauge and forecast overlap. It shifts the forecast by one constant and is not the Department of Health calculation.
  • The saved-forecast check keeps the first capture of each NOAA issue in this browser profile for 14 days. It requires a 3–36-hour-old path, no path gap over 12 hours and the observation nearest three hours earlier when it falls within 2.5–3.5 hours. The gauge change must be positive before “outpacing.” Clearing browser data clears this history; refreshes occur only while this tab is visible.
  • Rain forecasts more than 24 hours old are marked stale and excluded from the headline. Both points must cover the complete next 24 hours for a two-point total.
  • Missing values remain unavailable. Zero means a numeric zero was supplied.
  • A failed refresh removes the failed feed’s prior values. Feed failures do not turn into “low risk.”
